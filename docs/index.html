Pulling a clean signal
out of the noise.

A sensor is meant to report a smooth 50 Hz wave. What it actually records is buried under electrical hum and random noise. This page shows how a filter designed from first principles recovers the real signal, and lets you tune it yourself.

04 · Under the hood

How it works

For readers who want the engineering.

Method, in four steps
  1. The sensor is sampled 1000 times per second. A Hann-windowed FFT turns the recording into its frequency content: a 50 Hz tone, a 120 Hz tone and a flat noise floor.
  2. A low-pass FIR filter is designed with the windowed-sinc method: the ideal filter's impulse response is truncated to 101 taps and shaped with a Hamming window. The taps match scipy.signal.firwin to machine precision.
  3. The filter is applied by direct convolution. Because the taps are symmetric, every frequency is delayed by exactly 50 samples, so the output is shifted back to line up with the input.
  4. Quality is scored as a signal-to-noise ratio against the known clean signal. The jump from about 4 dB to about 16 dB is a 16x reduction in error power.
